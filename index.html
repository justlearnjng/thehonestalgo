Why this exists

TheHonestAlgo began with a simple mandate: build a trading system and test whether it can make money. It runs autonomously—checking the market every 30 minutes during trading hours, placing and closing its own simulated trades.

Then came the harder question: does it actually beat buying the S&P 500 and doing nothing?

A backtest spanning more than five years and 494 trades produced an uncomfortable answer—and a reason to publish the full record.

The strategy, in plain English

The system hunts for stocks already moving, then applies mechanical risk rules before a trade ever opens.

Momentum
60-day return in the top 20% of the S&P 500.
Volume
At least 1.5× normal volume—evidence that participation is expanding.
Breakout
Price within 5% of its 60-day high.
Risk
A 6% stop before entry; roughly 1.2% of the portfolio at risk per trade; six positions maximum.
Exit
30% profit target, trailing protection as winners run, and a 20-trading-day time limit.

High risk, but disciplined. Swing at real movers with quality behind them. No junk, no dead money.

The system made money. It nearly doubled the account. And it still lost to doing nothing.

This is the part most trading content leaves out: a strategy can look impressive until it is compared with the simplest alternative. Publishing that comparison is the point.

The baseline system went live while research continues on version two: smaller-cap momentum, post-earnings drift, and better bear-market behavior. The bar is explicit—beat SPY on a risk-adjusted basis, or admit it can’t be done.
